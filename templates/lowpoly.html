💡 What makes a good photo? ›

Works great

  • Clear subject with contrast — a face, an animal, a building. The subject should stand out clearly from the background.
  • Distinct color zones — areas of different color separated by clear edges (e.g. sky vs. mountains, face vs. hair).
  • Well-lit, in-focus — natural or even lighting, no strong blurs.
  • Colorful or tonal variety — images with several visible colors produce richer palettes.
  • At least 800 × 800 px — small images may lack detail for finer grids.

Avoid

  • Flat or monochromatic images — a gray wall, a plain sky, or anything where most pixels are nearly the same color. There isn't enough variation to build a meaningful palette.
  • Very fine textures — close-up hair, grass, sand, or fur. These create hundreds of tiny regions that are hard to color and look cluttered.
  • Very dark or overexposed photos — poor lighting hides the color differences the generator relies on.
  • Busy backgrounds — a crowd, dense foliage, or a detailed cityscape behind the subject can overwhelm the main shape.
